01 / ZOOLOGY

Animal Kingdom

Every phylum is really just seven yes/no questions answered differently: organization, symmetry, digestion, coelom, segmentation, circulation, and notochord. Learn the table once, and every phylum below just slots into it.

CharacterKey states to know
Level of organizationCellular (Porifera) → Tissue (Cnidaria) → Organ (Platyhelminthes+) → Organ system (Annelida onward)
SymmetryAsymmetrical (Porifera) → Radial (Cnidaria, adult Echinodermata) → Bilateral (Platyhelminthes onward)
DigestionIncomplete — one opening (Cnidaria, Platyhelminthes) vs Complete — mouth + anus (Aschelminthes onward)
CoelomAcoelomate (Platyhelminthes) → Pseudocoelomate (Aschelminthes) → Coelomate (Annelida onward)
CirculationOpen (Arthropoda, most Mollusca) vs Closed (Annelida, Cephalopod molluscs, Chordata)

Porifera

Level Cellular · Symmetry Asymmetrical · Pores Ostia + osculum
Sponges — Sycon, Spongilla (freshwater), Euspongia (bath sponge)

Cnidaria (Coelenterata)

Level Tissue · Symmetry Radial · Layers Diploblastic
Hydra, Obelia (shows metagenesis), Aurelia (jellyfish)

Platyhelminthes

Level Organ · Coelom Acoelomate · Digestion Incomplete
Planaria (high regeneration), Fasciola (liver fluke), Taenia (tapeworm)

Aschelminthes (Nematodes)

Coelom Pseudocoelomate · Digestion Complete
Ascaris (roundworm), Wuchereria (filariasis)

Annelida

Coelom True · Segmentation Metameric · Digestion Complete
Nereis, Pheretima (earthworm), Hirudinaria (leech)

Arthropoda

Circulation Open · Skeleton Chitinous exoskeleton
Apis (honeybee), Bombyx (silkworm), disease vectors: female Anopheles (malaria), Culex (filariasis), Aedes (dengue). Living fossil: Limulus

Mollusca

Circulation Open (closed in cephalopods) · Body Soft, unsegmented
Octopus, Pila (apple snail), Sepia (cuttlefish), Patella (limpet)

Echinodermata

Symmetry Radial (adult) · System Water-vascular
Antedon (sea lily), Asterias (starfish), Cucumaria (sea cucumber), Echinus (sea urchin)

Hemichordata

Non-chordate, but shares some chordate-like features
Balanoglossus (acorn worm)
Trick

Walk the ladder, not the list: instead of memorising nine phyla separately, memorise the seven-character table once — then for each phylum, just note which boxes flip. Symmetry flips at Cnidaria→Platyhelminthes; coelom flips twice (Platyhelminthes→Aschelminthes→Annelida); circulation flips at Annelida and again inside Mollusca (cephalopods).

02 / BOTANY

Plant Kingdom & Morphology

From algae to angiosperms, then into root, stem, leaf, and flower modifications — the examples that show up as one-line identification questions almost every year.

Algae

Green chlorophyll-dominant · Brown fucoxanthin · Red phycoerythrin
Green: Ulothrix, Spirogyra, Volvox · Brown: Dictyota, Sargassum · Red: Porphyra, Gracilaria · Chlorella — valued as a protein-rich food source

Bryophytes

"Amphibians of the plant kingdom" — need water for fertilisation
Funaria (common moss), Sphagnum (peaty moss — used as fuel & packing material)

Pteridophytes

First plants with true vascular tissue; three classes
Psilopsida, Lycopsida, Pteropsida — ferns and their relatives

Gymnosperms

"Naked seeds" — no fruit wall around the seed
Cycas (pinnate leaves, coralloid roots with N₂-fixing bacteria), Pinus (branched stem, mycorrhizal roots)

Angiosperms — Dicots vs Monocots

Dicot tap root, reticulate venation · Monocot fibrous root, parallel venation
Dicot: mustard family · Monocot: wheat, rice and other grasses

Root modifications

Storage, support, or extra function beyond anchorage
Tap root — storage in carrot; fibrous — wheat; sweet potato = a modified adventitious/tuberous root, not a stem (unlike potato)

Stem modifications

Underground stems store food and survive dormancy
Tuber — potato; rhizome — ginger; corm — Colocasia

Leaf modifications

Leaves repurposed for climbing, defence, or storage
Tendrils — grapevine; thorns — Bougainvillea; spines — cactus; flattened petiole (phyllode) in some Acacia

Flower symmetry & placentation

Actinomorphic (radial) vs Zygomorphic (bilateral) vs Asymmetric
Placentation types: axile, parietal, free-central, basal — describe where ovules attach inside the ovary
Trick

Root vs stem look-alikes: if it has nodes, internodes, or scale leaves — even underground — it's a stem (potato, ginger, colocasia). If it just swells and has no nodes, it's a root (carrot, sweet potato, radish).

Trick

Family mnemonics from class: the instructor's phrase for locking in Solanaceae's defining genera was "Delhi Public School." Treat each word as a peg for one genus/character from that family in your own notes — pegs you build yourself outlast ones you're just given.

03 / HUMAN & ANIMAL PHYSIOLOGY

Physiology in Numbers

The systems that get asked as "match the animal to the feature" — circulation, respiration, excretion, and the tissue types underneath them.

GroupHeart chambersCirculation
Fish2 (1 atrium, 1 ventricle)Single, closed
Amphibians & reptiles3 (mostly)Double, incompletely separated
Birds & mammals4Double, fully closed & separated
Pacemaker sequence

Conduction pathway

  • SA node — the natural pacemaker, ~75 beats/min
  • AV node — relays the impulse next
  • Bundle of His → Purkinje fibres — spread it through the ventricles
Respiration by group

Gas exchange surfaces

  • Simple organisms — direct body-surface exchange
  • Fish — gills
  • Amphibians, reptiles, birds, mammals — lungs
  • Insects — a tracheal system, bypassing blood entirely
Excretion

Waste type & organ

  • Ammonotelic — ammonia (most aquatic animals)
  • Ureotelic — urea (mammals)
  • Uricotelic — uric acid (birds, reptiles, insects)
  • Organs: nephridia (annelids), Malpighian tubules (insects), kidneys (vertebrates)
Tissues

Plant vs animal building blocks

  • Simple permanent (plant): parenchyma, collenchyma, sclerenchyma
  • Complex (plant): xylem (tracheids, vessels), phloem (sieve tubes, companion cells)
  • Connective tissue: tendon = muscle→bone, ligament = bone→bone
Trick

Tendon vs ligament: "Tendon has a T, like muscle-T-issue" — tendons anchor muscle to bone; ligaments (bone-to-bone) are the ones stabilising joints.

04 / GENETICS & MOLECULAR BIOLOGY

Inheritance, Molecules & Evolution

Mendel's laws, the exceptions to them, the molecules underneath DNA, and the evidence trail evolution leaves behind.

Inheritance patterns

Beyond simple dominance

  • Incomplete dominance — blended phenotype (neither parent trait shows fully)
  • Codominance — both alleles show fully at once, e.g. ABO blood group
  • Multiple allelism — more than two alleles exist for one gene in the population, as with ABO
Sex determination

Two systems

  • XX–XY — humans and most mammals; male is heterogametic
  • ZW–ZZ — birds and some reptiles/fish; female is heterogametic
Genetic disorders

Know the mechanism, not just the name

  • Sickle cell anaemia — single amino-acid substitution in haemoglobin
  • Down syndrome — trisomy of chromosome 21
  • Haemophilia & colour blindness — X-linked recessive
Evolution evidence

Homology vs analogy

  • Homologous organs — same origin, different function (e.g. forelimbs across vertebrates) → divergent evolution
  • Analogous organs — different origin, same function (e.g. bat wing vs insect wing) → convergent evolution
  • Classic examples: Darwin's finches (adaptive radiation), industrial melanism in peppered moths (natural selection in real time)
Trick

Homologous vs analogous, fast: "Homologous = same origin." Two m's, one memory hook — everything else follows from that.

05 / MICROBIOLOGY & IMMUNITY

Microbes, Blood & Defence

The microbes that keep appearing in application questions, and the immune-system basics that connect straight to human health chapters.

Fermentation & food microbes

Bacteria and fungi doing useful chemistry
Lactobacillus (curd), Saccharomyces (bread, beer)

Nitrogen-fixers & antibiotics

Agriculture and medicine's microbial workhorses
Rhizobium, Azotobacter (N-fixation); Penicillium (penicillin)

Blood cells

White cell family, at a glance
Neutrophils, eosinophils, basophils, lymphocytes, monocytes — each with a distinct immune role

Blood groups & immunity

Universal donor: O · Universal recipient: AB
Active immunity — body makes its own antibodies (infection/vaccine); passive immunity — ready-made antibodies given directly
Trick

Active vs passive, in one line: Active = your own factory (slower, but lasting). Passive = an import (fast, but temporary) — think of antibodies given after a snakebite.

06 / ECOLOGY & BIODIVERSITY

Ecosystems, Adaptation & Conservation

The species-interaction vocabulary and the conservation numbers NEET likes to test as direct recall.

Ecosystem roles

Who does what

  • Producers — fix energy (plants, algae)
  • Consumers — primary (herbivores), secondary (carnivores) and beyond
  • Decomposers — recycle nutrients back into the system
Species interactions

Who benefits, who doesn't

  • Mutualism — both benefit
  • Parasitism — one benefits, one harmed
  • Competition — both lose access to a shared resource; see Gause's competitive exclusion principle
Adaptation & behaviour

Surviving the extremes

  • Desert adaptations vs cold-climate adaptations
  • Hibernation — winter dormancy; aestivation — summer dormancy; migration — seasonal relocation
Biodiversity numbers

Genetic diversity, quantified

  • Rice — roughly 50,000 varieties
  • Mango — roughly 1,000 varieties in India
  • Conservation: biosphere reserves, national parks, wildlife sanctuaries, seed banks
Trick

Hibernation vs aestivation: "Hibernation" sounds cold like December; aestivation shares its root with "estival," summer — so aestivation is the summer sleep.

07 / BIOTECHNOLOGY

Tools, Products & Medical Applications

The toolkit chapter — enzymes and vectors first, then what they're actually used to build.

Restriction enzymes

Molecular scissors, sequence-specific
HindII, EcoRI — cut DNA to create the fragments used in cloning

Cloning vectors

The delivery vehicles for foreign DNA
Plasmids, bacteriophages — carry the cut gene into a host cell

Transgenic products

Foreign genes, deliberately inserted for a trait
Golden rice (beta-carotene), Bt cotton (pest resistance)

Assisted reproduction

Know the difference, not just the acronym
IVF — fertilisation happens outside the body; GIFT — gametes transferred into the fallopian tube; artificial insemination — semen introduced directly, fertilisation still happens inside
Screening & diagnostics

Prenatal & disease testing

  • Amniocentesis — screens for genetic/chromosomal disorders prenatally
  • Key pathogens to know by disease: HIV, Hepatitis, Syphilis, Gonorrhea
Trick

IVF vs GIFT, one detail: if fertilisation happens in the lab, it's IVF ("in vitro" = in glass). If it happens back inside the body, it's GIFT — the gametes are the gift, not the embryo.
