01 · ATOMIC RADIUS

Size of the atom

Across a period, radius generally decreases as effective nuclear charge rises. Down a group, radius generally increases because new shells are added.

→ period: ↓   |   group: ↑
Zeff + SHELLS
01E · EXCEPTION

Al > Ga

Gallium is below aluminium, yet their sizes are unexpectedly close and the lecture highlights Al as larger. Poor shielding by filled 3d electrons in Ga raises effective nuclear attraction.

Al radius > Ga radius
3d SHIELDING
01D · 3d SERIES

Sc → Zn is not smooth

Size decreases from Sc toward Cr, shows a Mn irregularity, stays nearly similar through Fe–Ni, then increases toward Cu/Zn as electron–electron repulsion becomes important.

Sc ↓ Cr ↗ Mn ≈ Fe ≈ Co ≈ Ni ↗ Cu/Zn
TRANSITION TREND
01E · FAMOUS

4d ≈ 5d

Elements in the same group of the 4d and 5d series can have remarkably similar sizes. Lanthanide contraction pulls 5d atoms inward.

4d size ≈ 5d size
LANTHANIDE CONTRACTION
02 · IONIZATION

Holding an electron

Ionization enthalpy is the energy required to remove an electron from a gaseous atom. Higher Zeff and smaller size generally mean stronger hold.

period ↑   |   group ↓
SIZE · Zeff
02E · PERIOD 2

B < Be

Be has a filled 2s subshell. B loses its higher-energy 2p electron more easily, so B has lower ionization enthalpy than Be.

Be: 2s²   |   B: 2p¹
PENETRATION
02E · PERIOD 2

O < N

N has a stable half-filled 2p³ arrangement. O introduces paired electrons in one p orbital, increasing repulsion and making removal easier.

N: 2p³   |   O: 2p⁴
HALF-FILLED
02E · GROUP 13

The “W” trend

Group 13 ionization enthalpy does not fall smoothly. Penetration, poor shielding and contraction effects compete with the expected size trend.

B → Al → Ga → In → Tl : irregular
GROUP 13
03 · ELECTRON GAIN

Adding an electron

Electron gain enthalpy measures the energy change when an atom gains an electron. Smaller atoms can have unexpectedly stronger repulsions.

Cl > F · S > O (magnitude)
REPULSION
03E · FAMOUS

Cl beats F

Fluorine is tiny, so the incoming electron enters a very compact 2p region with strong electron–electron repulsion. Chlorine’s larger 3p region accommodates it better.

EA: Cl > F
SMALL-SIZE REPULSION
03E · FAMOUS

S beats O

The same compact-orbital repulsion idea makes sulfur’s electron gain more favorable than oxygen’s.

EA: S > O
2p vs 3p
04 · ELECTRONEGATIVITY

Pulling shared electrons

Electronegativity generally increases across a period and decreases down a group. It also rises with greater s-character in hybrid orbitals.

sp > sp² > sp³
s-CHARACTER
05 · IONIC RADIUS

Charge changes size

Cations are smaller than their parent atoms; anions are larger. More positive charge contracts the ion; more negative charge expands electron–electron repulsion.

cation: ↓   |   anion: ↑
ION SIZE
06D · D-BLOCK

Zn, Cd, Hg: low mp

These metals have filled d¹⁰ configurations and no d-electron contribution from unpaired electrons to strengthen metallic bonding in the usual transition-metal way.

d¹⁰ → weaker bonding → lower mp
FULL d-SHELL
07D · D-BLOCK

Density: Sc → Zn

Density generally rises toward Cu because mass increases while size changes modestly. It then falls at Zn as the volume increase becomes significant.

Sc ↑ → Cu ↓ → Zn
MASS / VOLUME
08D · CONFIGURATION

Cr & Cu

Cr and Cu show anomalous ground-state configurations because half-filled d⁵ and fully filled d¹⁰ arrangements provide extra stability.

Cr: [Ar] 3d⁵4s¹ · Cu: [Ar] 3d¹⁰4s¹
CONFIGURATION
09E · GROUP 13

Boron vs Gallium mp

Boron has an extended covalent network with B₁₂ structural units, giving it a very high melting point. Gallium has an unusually low melting point.

B: very high mp · Ga: very low mp
COVALENT NETWORK
10E · GROUP 13

BF₃ & back bonding

Halogen lone-pair donation into boron’s vacant p orbital reduces electron deficiency. Back bonding changes the Lewis acidity pattern and is a classic conceptual trap.

X lone pair → B vacant p

Rapid glossary

Words you must understand before memorising exceptions.

Zeff

Effective nuclear charge actually felt by an electron after shielding.

Shielding

Inner electrons reduce the nucleus’s attraction for an outer electron.

Penetration

How closely an orbital electron density approaches the nucleus; s penetrates most.

Repulsion

Electron–electron crowding can destabilise compact orbitals and change trends.

Half-filled

Symmetric arrangements such as p³ or d⁵ receive extra stability.

Lanthanide contraction

Poor 4f shielding causes contraction across lanthanides, shrinking later 5d atoms.

Back bonding

Lone-pair donation from a ligand into an electron-deficient centre’s vacant orbital.

s-character

More s-character holds bonding electrons closer to the nucleus and increases electronegativity.
